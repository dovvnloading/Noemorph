# Cross-cultural mind, self, and awareness acquisition proposals

## Purpose and evidential status

This batch proposes 26 additions for expert acquisition review after screening all 577 records in the local catalog. It does **not** modify the catalog, assert that every item is a theory of phenomenal consciousness, or license cross-tradition synthesis. The batch includes philosophical positions, historical construct families, anthropological models, social accounts of selfhood, and disability-led frameworks because each can clarify a different part of the project's mind/self/awareness landscape.

Every proposal is marked either:

- **Promotion-ready**: at least two exact primary, authoritative, or scholarly locators; a `proposed_catalog_kind` from the application's `RECORD_KINDS` allowlist; a bounded claim; and no unresolved translation or community-governance blocker.
- **Backlog-only**: a worthwhile acquisition lead that still lacks an independent source, passage-level primary support, verified metadata, specialist translation review, lineage review, or community review. It must not be converted directly into a catalog record.

There are 8 promotion-ready proposals and 18 backlog-only proposals. “Promotion-ready” is a source and schema threshold, not a claim that interpretation is final. Normal human review and catalog validation still apply.

## Screening method

The complete catalog was retrieved and normalized for title, aliases, author or tradition, and scope. Candidate names were checked for exact and near matches. Neighbor records were then inspected to distinguish a genuinely missing position from a narrower or broader version already present. Sources were selected from primary texts or translations, university-press scholarship, peer-reviewed articles, discipline reference works, and community or lineage sources where provenance required them.

## Non-equivalence and attribution rules

1. **Translation is evidence, not cleanup.** Preserve source-language terms where English glosses would prejudge ontology. Store alternative translations and the exact edition or translator. Do not use a shared English word such as “mind,” “self,” “emptiness,” “spirit,” or “awareness” as evidence of a shared construct.
2. **Internal contestation stays visible.** A school, region, language, or people is not a single speaker. Store rival interpretations and historical phases as attributed claims, not averaged summaries.
3. **Descriptive and normative claims stay separate.** Anthropological accounts, ethical ideals, clinical hypotheses, and metaphysical positions answer different questions. Similarity detection must not flatten these levels.
4. **Community knowledge and analyst models stay separate.** Hózhó is not Descola's typology; Runa statements are not identical to Kohn's Peircean analysis. Record who is speaking, from which method, and with what authority.
5. **Similarity never proves influence or equivalence.** Embedding proximity can generate a review candidate only. Historical lineage requires textual or archival evidence; conceptual equivalence requires argument and specialist review.
6. **Sacred and restricted knowledge is out of scope.** Public scholarship does not authorize extracting ceremonial, clan-specific, lineage-restricted, or community-governed knowledge.
7. **Disability-led theories retain support needs.** Reciprocal or diversity-based accounts must not be edited into claims that impairment, disability, or the need for care and accommodation is unreal.

## Required next review steps

For promotion-ready items:

1. Convert the proposal to full catalog schema without changing its bounded title or non-equivalence note.
2. Import exact citation metadata from the recorded locator and add passage or page locators for every substantive claim.
3. Run the normal catalog validator and duplicate audit.
4. Require one human review for attribution and one domain review for interpretive scope before publication.

For backlog-only items:

1. Resolve every listed `promotion_blocker`.
2. Obtain a passage-level primary source or a second independent scholarly locator where missing.
3. Conduct primary-language and translation review for Arabic, Sanskrit, Pāli, Tibetan, Chinese, Korean, Japanese, Akan, Yorùbá, and Diné terms as applicable.
4. Seek lineage or community review when a proposal concerns living traditions or Indigenous knowledge.
5. Promote only after the same evidence and schema gate used for the ready subset is satisfied.

No relationship edges, inferred lineages, or cross-tradition equivalences should be generated from this proposal batch. Those belong to a later, separately reviewed synthesis phase.
