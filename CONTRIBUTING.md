# Contributing to Noemorph

Researchers, developers, designers, and readers are welcome. Corrections to a
source, clearer boundaries, missing perspectives, accessibility improvements,
and code fixes are all valuable. Open an issue to discuss a substantial change,
or submit a focused pull request with enough context for another person to review it.

## Research contributions

- Give a stable primary or authoritative source and an exact locator for each
  substantive claim. Explain what the source supports and what it does not.
- Distinguish a theory, a measure, a historical tradition, and a challenge or
  argument. Describe the scope and limits of a record without presenting an
  unsettled interpretation as consensus.
- Respect living traditions, translation limits, and community attribution.
  Identify uncertainties and invite corrections from relevant communities.
- Do not infer relationships between theories from classification or embedding
  similarity. Relationship proposals require direct evidence and independent review.
- Write original summaries; do not submit full papers, scraped passages, images,
  or other third-party material without the right to redistribute it. See
  [THIRD_PARTY.md](THIRD_PARTY.md).

## Development

Start with the setup and verification instructions in [README.md](README.md).
Keep changes narrow, describe the problem and approach in the pull request, and
include the checks you ran. If changing the catalog, run the catalog tests and
identify any source or provenance changes. Some local research workflows need
optional third-party source material or generated caches that are not included
in a public checkout; explain any verification you could not run.

## License and participation

By submitting an original contribution, you agree that it may be distributed
under the project's MIT license. Only submit material you have the right to
contribute. Be respectful, discuss ideas on their merits, and assume good faith.
Report sensitive security concerns privately to the repository owner rather than
posting exploit details in a public issue.
